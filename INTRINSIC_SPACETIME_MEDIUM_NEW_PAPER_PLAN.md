# Intrinsic Spacetime Medium as an Alternative to Galactic DM Phenomenology

Date: 2026-02-17

## 0) Goal (one sentence)
Develop a **fully falsifiable** intrinsic-spacetime-mechanics explanation for galaxy “halo” phenomenology—framed as an **effective geometric/medium response** to baryons—then test it systematically on SPARC-scale data (and clearly state what is and is not addressed: e.g., lensing, clusters, cosmology).

## 1) Core framing (pipeline language, ontology)
### Two interpretations of the same fitted residual
Given a baryonic prediction and an observed rotation curve, the pipeline fits a residual extra term:
- $g_{\rm obs}(R) = g_{\rm bar}(R) + g_{\rm extra}(R)$.

Interpretations:
- **Standard CDM (halo):** $g_{\rm extra}$ is sourced by an additional mass component $\rho_{\rm DM}$ within standard gravity.
- **Intrinsic medium:** $g_{\rm extra}$ is an **effective geometric response** of spacetime/connection to the same baryonic source (GR-as-EFT / constitutive behavior), without an extra mass component.

This paper is about making the *intrinsic medium* interpretation precise enough to be predictive and falsifiable.

### Parsimony: what “simpler” really means here
“More parsimonious” is not “more familiar”; it is mainly:
- **Fewer degrees of freedom** per galaxy (ideally 0–2 parameters, not an arbitrary function),
- **Tighter cross-domain constraints** (same mechanism must not break lensing, solar-system tests, clusters, cosmology, etc., if you claim metric equivalence),
- **Better out-of-sample prediction** of residual structure from internal observables.

Within *standard GR*, any successful alternative to CDM has to supply the missing curvature via some effective source term (an extra component of $T_{\mu\nu}$, or equivalently a constitutive/response relation that generates an effective stress–energy). The “materials reverse engineering” stance is:
- do **not** assume particulate, collisionless CDM by default;
- instead infer what *effective* stress–energy/response would be required and ask whether it can be generated by a small, physically motivated closure tied to baryons.

Important language discipline for the paper:
- Avoid claiming we know spacetime is literally a fluid/solid.
- Prefer **EFT/constitutive** wording: “effective medium response”, “closure relation”, “boundary layer”, “auxiliary field”, “polarization-like stress”, etc.

## 2) Hypotheses to compare (what we are actually testing)
We want crisp hypotheses with discriminants.

### H0: “DM halo” baseline (phenomenological)
- Residuals are explained by an additional mass profile.
- Prediction structure: flexible; environment dependence enters via astrophysical correlation, not necessarily fundamental.

### H1: Intrinsic spacetime medium (internal/boundary-layer response)
- Residual amplitude/shape is mainly set by **internal structure and edge/boundary-layer conditions**.
- Environment effects are allowed but expected to be **secondary/indirect** (via baryon evolution: stripping, quenching, morphology).

### H2: Explicit environment-responsive geometry (TPT/OSA family)
- Residuals are **directly modulated** by external density contrast / environment embedding at fixed internal controls.
- Strong discriminant: environment-stratified signatures that survive distance/quality mixing controls.

### Discriminants (what would count as evidence)
- **Intrinsic wins** if out-of-sample prediction of edge amplitude improves primarily with internal features (composition/structure) rather than environment, and environment signals fail robust stratified nulls.
- **Env-responsive wins** if a robust environment dependence persists after:
  - internal scale controls,
  - partial correlation (residualize BOTH target and env),
  - permutation tests,
  - stratified permutations (Q_flag, distance, etc.),
  - and ideally alternative environment proxies.

## 3) Minimal intrinsic-medium model spec (start as simple as possible)
We need a model that:
- produces an outer-tail contribution without inserting it “by hand”,
- links the effect to boundary-layer/transition structure,
- has a small number of parameters, ideally 0–2 per galaxy.

### Phenomenology-first (inverse-GR) construction (what “do it anyway” means)
Start from what is measured (rotation curves) and infer the *effective geometry/source* required **within GR**, then restrict it by a low-DOF closure.

1) **Observed kinematics → required radial force**
- For an approximately circular orbit in a stationary, weak-field galaxy potential, define
  - $g_{\rm obs}(R) \equiv v_{\rm obs}^2(R)/R$.
- Compute baryonic prediction $g_{\rm bar}(R)$ from SPARC components.
- Define the residual $g_{\rm extra}(R) \equiv g_{\rm obs}(R)-g_{\rm bar}(R)$.

2) **Residual force → effective potential(s)**
- In Newtonian gauge (weak field), write two scalar potentials $(\Phi,\Psi)$ in
  - $ds^2=-(1+2\Phi)dt^2+(1-2\Psi)\,d\mathbf{x}^2$.
- Nonrelativistic dynamics mostly probes $\Phi$ (through $\nabla\Phi$).
- Lensing probes roughly $(\Phi+\Psi)$.

3) **Effective potentials → effective stress–energy**
- In GR, specifying potentials (or equivalently $g_{\rm extra}$) implies an *effective* source term beyond the observed baryons.
- The paper’s move is: interpret that extra source as an intrinsic response (a constitutive “polarization stress”), not particulate CDM, and then constrain it.

4) **Restrict the inverse freedom (parsimony constraint)**
- The inverse problem is underdetermined unless we impose a closure:
  - locality/nonlocality choice,
  - dependence only on baryonic invariants and a small number of constants,
  - boundary-layer activation near a transition (e.g., $g_{\rm bar}\sim a_0$),
  - stability/energy conditions as optional priors.

This explicitly operationalizes “think outside the box” while staying exam-grade: we are not claiming microscopic composition, we are inferring the *minimum effective response* and then trying to compress it.

### Option A: Auxiliary-field boundary-layer tail (operational model)
Introduce an auxiliary scalar/field $\chi$ coupled to baryons such that:
- near a transition radius $R_t$ (e.g., where $g_{\rm bar}\sim a_0$), $\chi$ is sourced (or activated),
- outside the source region, $\chi'(R)\propto 1/R$ so $g_{\rm extra}\propto 1/R$ and $v_{\rm extra}\approx$ constant.

This matches what we already implemented as a toy pipeline (edge-amplitude proxy): it is a **mechanism sketch** for an intrinsic response.

#### Minimal covariant sketch (enough to be concrete, not overcommitted)
One simple “GR + response field” template is:
- Action $S=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,\big[\frac{1}{16\pi G}R + \mathcal{L}_{\rm bar}(g,\psi) + \mathcal{L}_\chi(g,\chi) + \mathcal{L}_{\rm int}(g,\chi,\psi)\big]$.
- Choose $\mathcal{L}_\chi$ so that the static, axisymmetric weak-field equation admits an approximately conserved radial flux outside an activation region, yielding $\chi'\sim 1/R$.
- Choose $\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}$ so activation is tied to a baryonic edge/transition invariant (e.g., a function of $|\nabla\Phi_{\rm bar}|/a_0$ or a surface-density proxy), limiting per-galaxy freedom.

This preserves “standard GR” on the left-hand side while allowing the right-hand side to contain an intrinsic response stress–energy.

### Option B: EFT-motivated constitutive relation (paper-facing)
Write an effective closure in weak field:
- $\Phi = \Phi_{\rm bar} + \Phi_{\rm resp}$,
- $\Phi_{\rm resp}$ depends on invariants of $\Phi_{\rm bar}$ and its gradients in a boundary layer (e.g., $|\nabla\Phi_{\rm bar}|/a_0$, curvature scales).

Keep it minimal: 1–2 dimensionless constants + one activation scale.

## 4) Predictions we can test now (rotation-curve domain)
### Shape-level predictions
- Outer tail approximately $g_{\rm extra}\sim 1/R$ beyond the activation region.
- Transition tied to where $g_{\rm bar}\sim a_0$ (or a nearby invariant).

### Amplitude-level predictions
- The fitted amplitude (your `v_extra_asym_kms` proxy) should correlate most strongly with:
  - internal edge/boundary indicators (e.g., baryonic surface density drop, gas fraction near $R_t$, disk scale length measures),
  - not strongly with distance artifacts,
  - and only weakly/indirectly with environment proxies.

### Environment prediction (intrinsic-medium stance)
- Any environment dependence should become weak/non-robust once distance/quality and key internal covariates are controlled.

### Hard falsification hooks (what would actually kill the idea)
- **Compression failure**: if $g_{\rm extra}(R)$ cannot be summarized by a universal low-DOF shape/amplitude tied to baryons (needs essentially a free function per galaxy), the “materials reverse engineering” story collapses into curve-fitting.
- **Slip/lensing inconsistency** (if metric-equivalence is claimed): if the inferred extra potential needed for dynamics implies a $(\Phi,\Psi)$ pair that cannot match lensing without adding new freedom, the claim must be weakened or revised.
- **Out-of-sample failure**: if internal predictors do not improve prediction of the edge statistic in CV beyond scale controls, the “intrinsic boundary-layer” causal story is not supported.

## 5) Empirical validation ladder (what “robust” means in this paper)
This is the part that makes the paper exam-ready.

### Step 1: Data + reproducibility
- Precisely define inclusion criteria and missing-data handling.
- One command regenerates:
  - the per-galaxy fitted summaries,
  - the joined environment columns,
  - the analysis tables.

### Step 2: Baseline fits and residual definitions
- Define one “edge amplitude” measure (primary) and 1–2 alternates (sensitivity).
- Define BASE controls (scale): log(Vflat), log(Rdisk) (and optionally log(D)).

### Step 3: Inference that does not rely on fragile p-values
- Permutation p-values (two-tailed) for Pearson and Spearman.
- Partial-correlation mode: residualize BOTH y and env/feature on controls.

### Step 4: Stratified permutation stress tests
- Stratify by Q_flag.
- Stratify by distance quantiles.
- Stratify by Q_flag × distance quantiles.
- (Optional) stratify by coarse morphology.

### Step 5: Out-of-sample prediction (decisive)
- Repeated k-fold CV:
  - baseline = scale-only,
  - extended = scale + intrinsic-structure features,
  - compare with/without environment features.

Report:
- mean/median CV RMSE (or MAE),
- nested-model tests (or permutation-based model comparison),
- robustness to filtering Q_flag==1.

### Step 6: Negative controls / sanity checks
- Replace env column with shuffled env; confirm significance disappears.
- Use distance-only as a “confounder detector.”
