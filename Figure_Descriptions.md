# Figure descriptions (draft outline)

## Figure 1 — Galactic boundary-layer geometry

**What the figure shows**

- A schematic cross-section of a disk galaxy indicating the baryon-dense interior (bulge + disk), a transition region where baryonic gradients weaken, and the diffuse exterior environment.
- A highlighted “boundary-layer” band where nonlinear/response effects are hypothesized to become important.
- A marked radius where the characteristic acceleration drops to $a(R)\sim a_0$ (empirical low-acceleration threshold).

**Rationale**

- Establishes the geometric precondition for a boundary-layer / intrinsic-response interpretation: galaxies exhibit a natural interior–exterior interface where weak-field linearization may cease to be uniformly valid.

**What it supports**

- Motivates why the outskirts are a natural regime for higher-order curvature terms, nonlocal response, quasi-harmonic modes, or long-range tails to emerge.

## Figure 2 — Harmonic / quasi-harmonic correction to the potential

**What the figure shows**

- A comparison between a baseline Newtonian potential $\Phi_N(r)$ and an augmented potential
	$$\Phi(r)=\Phi_N(r)+A\cos(kr+\delta)\,e^{-r/L}.$$
- The corresponding acceleration modification at large radii, with the oscillatory envelope and the decay length $L$ indicated.

**Rationale**

- Illustrates a concrete mathematical template by which intrinsic metric structure could generate acceleration enhancements in the low-acceleration regime, without introducing particulate dark matter.

**What it supports**

- Makes “harmonic modes in the metric/response sector” operational: a small correction can have an outsized dynamical impact where $g$ is small.

## Figure 3 — Rotation curves with and without boundary-layer contribution

**What the figure shows**

- A standard rotation-curve panel comparing:
	- observed $v_{\rm obs}(r)$,
	- baryons-only prediction (declining $v\propto r^{-1/2}$ in the simplest regime),
	- baryons + boundary-layer/response contribution.

**Rationale**

- Provides the primary rotation-curve-domain comparison that anchors the hypothesis in a familiar observable.

**What it supports**

- Demonstrates whether the added term yields the correct outer behavior and whether the improvement is smooth and non-pathological over the data range.

## Figure 4 — Lensing mass vs baryonic mass (cluster-scale; conceptual)

**What the figure shows**

- A stylized representation of baryonic tracers, a lensing reconstruction, and a centroid offset.
- An overlay illustrating how an extended metric/curvature response could shift the effective lensing centroid.

**Rationale**

- Lensing is often taken as decisive evidence for dark matter; this figure frames the alternative hypothesis that lensing responds to the metric rather than directly to matter.

**What it supports**

- Communicates (at a conceptual level) how extended curvature structure could mimic halo-like lensing features and produce offsets.

## Figure 5 — Boundary-layer differential-equation behavior (optional)

**What the figure shows**

- Representative solutions of a driven linear equation (as a proxy for boundary-layer response), e.g.
	$$\frac{d^2 f}{dr^2}-k^2 f=S(r),$$
	illustrating Yukawa-like exponentials, oscillatory solutions, and mixed modes.

**Rationale**

- Provides mathematical intuition for how localized source terms can produce large-scale structure outside an activation region.

**What it supports**

- Argues that the qualitative behavior is generic for boundary-layer systems and is not reliant on fine-tuning.

## Figure 6 — Emergence of an acceleration scale $a_0\sim cH_0$ (optional)

**What the figure shows**

- A conceptual diagram linking the empirical scale $a_0$ and the cosmological scale $cH_0$, indicating how EFT corrections or response-sector scales could naturally introduce a low-acceleration threshold.

**Rationale**

- Reinforces the motivation for treating $a_0$ as a physically meaningful scale rather than an arbitrary fit parameter.

**What it supports**

- Provides a bridge from galactic phenomenology to broader-scale considerations (while remaining explicit about scope limits).
